Interfaces often compress uncertainty into a scalar: a percentage, a colored badge, a confidence bar. The compression is useful, but it hides the reason for the doubt.

## Doubt has a direction

One claim may be uncertain because the evidence is thin. Another may be well supported in one setting and fragile outside it. A third may depend on a definition that two readers do not share.

These are not interchangeable states. A better interface could pair uncertainty with its direction:

- missing evidence;
- contested premise;
- scope boundary;
- unresolved measurement;
- plausible alternative explanation.

This vocabulary would not remove ambiguity. It could make ambiguity discussable.

The open question is whether extra structure genuinely improves reasoning, or merely gives uncertainty a more polished costume.
