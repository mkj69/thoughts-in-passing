An archive and a garden can contain the same things. Both preserve fragments from the past. Both reward a careful naming system. Both become more valuable as they accumulate history. Yet they ask different questions.

An archive asks: *How can this remain findable?*

A garden asks: *What deserves attention now?*

## Attention changes the collection

The garden metaphor matters because it makes maintenance part of the work. Notes are not inert records. Some need pruning. Some should be connected across a gap. Some are allowed to remain seeds for a long time without pretending to be conclusions.

Backlinks help because they show how later ideas reinterpret earlier ones. Maturity labels help because they resist the visual sameness of a chronological feed. Neither feature makes a garden by itself. The garden appears only when the author returns.

That is why a digital garden should not optimize solely for capture. Capture makes a larger archive. A living collection also needs rituals of revisiting: wandering, resurfacing, linking, and occasionally letting an idea go.
